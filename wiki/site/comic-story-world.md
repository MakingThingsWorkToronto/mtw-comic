A reader opens an issue knowing nothing. Within the first page they must be able to answer, from the boxes alone: **where are we, when is it, whose story is this, what do they want, and what is going wrong.** Everything else on this page exists so that a story can answer those five on page one.

## The place: Toronto, the Keon Tower

Every issue happens in one real city, **Toronto**, and most of it in one invented building: the **Keon Tower**, a 40-storey glass office tower on **King Street West** (named after Dave Keon, by the hockey naming rule). Putting the cast's employers in the same building is what lets their stories cross: a disaster on one floor reaches the others by morning, and "three floors down" means something.

| Place | Who is there | What it looks like (art cue) |
|---|---|---|
| **King Street West, at night** | Establishing shot | Wet street, streetcar lines, the tower lit floor by floor; teal is the only clean light |
| **Floor 31: Sittler Business Services** | Marco Lemieux, sales | Open-plan sales floor, glass meeting rooms, laptops and coffee |
| **Floor 28: Bourque & Coffey LLP** | Dana Dryden, legal | Small practice: file rooms, binders, one window office |
| **Floor 19: Esposito Power & Light, training office** | Sam Howe, training | Safety posters, a hard hat on the desk, a training room with a projector |
| **Floor 12: the Dionne Foundation** | Ruth Orr, grants | Quiet, carpeted, stacks of applications in labelled trays |
| **Floor 28, inside:** Ryan Potvin's glass office, the partners' boardroom, the elevator lobby | Issue #3 | Glass walls, the boardroom's long table, the lobby where people talk where they shouldn't |
| **Basement one (B1): Bourque & Coffey's records room** | Dana, Issue #4; the firm's old document system, Plante | Shelves of boxes, a green-screen terminal, a binder manual with pencil notes |
| **Basement two (B2): the server room** | The building's systems; where agents run | Racks, cable trays, the hum; the Rogue Agent's ground |
| **The lobby and the elevators** | Where the cast meet | Marble, a security desk, three elevator banks |
| **The roof** | The issue's last look at the city | The skyline, the CN Tower, dawn coming up over the lake |
| **The second brain** | Drawn behind the heroes | The tower's teal double: a lattice of floors that light up one at a time as people give answers |

Places outside the tower are used when an issue needs them, and are always named in a place caption the first time. So far: **Esposito's substation yard in Etobicoke** (transformers, bucket trucks, a site trailer; Issue #2).

**Who knows whom in the tower:** Bourque & Coffey are Sittler's lawyers, and the Dionne Foundation and Esposito Power & Light are Sittler customers, which is why one bad night on floor 31 reaches the whole building. Matt is on the tower's after-hours call list. In Issue 1 Dana gives Marco the contact when Sittler's contract job sends the wrong document; Matt arrives to help contain that job. In Issue 4 Dana calls him only after the week-five access report, not during the initial planning.

## The time: autumn 2026

The series is set in the present day, from **autumn 2026** into the winter, and each issue states its day and time. Clock captions carry the pace.

| Issue | POV | When | Where it opens |
|---|---|---|---|
| #1 The night shift. | Marco | Thursday 15 October 2026, 11:04 p.m., to Monday 19 October, 9:10 a.m. | Floor 31, Marco's desk |
| #2 The keys to production. | Sam | Thursday 5 November 2026, 5:00 p.m., to Monday 9 November, 6:00 a.m. | Esposito Power & Light, floor 19 |

An issue keeps one clock. Every scene change says how much time has passed ("12:40 a.m.", "Friday.", "Week three."), and flashbacks are marked as such in their caption.

## Meeting the cast

A reader meets each person once per issue, on their first appearance, in three ways at the same time:

1. **An introduction caption** in that box: the name, what they do and where, and one line of character. It uses the branded teal `#1a9ba1` at the existing `0.85` caption fill opacity (Matt, 2026-10-09), set apart from the yellow/cream scene and narration captions. Story data marks it `characterIntro: true`; later narration merely mentioning a name stays narration.
2. **A signature they can't miss**, kept on every page (the [13 visual storytelling tips](https://13thdimension.com/13-visual-storytelling-tips-for-comics/): signature design elements).
3. **An action that shows the character line** in the same or next box, so the caption is proved, not just claimed.

No more than two people are introduced on one page. A character who has not been introduced does not speak.

**People seen but never speaking** need no caption, only a name where it matters: **George Armstrong** (Issue #4) built Plante, Bourque & Coffey's document system since 1996, retired in 2019, and appears only in a photo and his pencil notes in the manual. Names come from hockey (Mikita, Potvin, Armstrong, and Plante after the goalie).

**Villains enter through the damage they do**, without a roll call or a villain title card. Matt's 2026-10-09 exception gives the embodied red rogue agent an identifying teal caption: name it as an AI and explain its actual job and unchecked access, on a box that depicts it. Home uses "The rogue agent. Sittler’s contract AI. It sends without asking." Other issues use the agent's own task. Metaphorical villains retain their existing damage-first presentation.

Resolve each character's current dedicated sheet from `wiki/comics-library/characters.json` → `modelRecord`, then the image manifest's declared treatment. Matt, Claude and Codex retain their established dedicated sheets. Ruth and Joan have separate appearances; human operators and digital agents have individually mapped references. Shared sheets and all earlier references remain retained provenance; do not substitute a shared sheet when a dedicated mapping exists. A missing mapping is an integration gap, so check registered and staged owner packets before generating another sheet. ## Words a reader will meet

| Word | What it means in the story | How it is shown |
|---|---|---|
| **Chat** | The AI tools everyone uses without checking | Phones and laptops, a cheerful green tick |
| **Steered** | An AI given human judgement | The hero's light turns teal |
| **The gate** | The check a person owns before work goes out | A person stopping work at a door, a desk, a signature |
| **Operators** | The people who own the work and hold the gates | Marco, Dana, Ruth, Sam once they start checking |
| **An owner** | The person who answers for an agent, a link or a file | An access form whose Reason and Owner fields are blank until someone fills them (Issues #2 and #3) |
| **The second brain** | The retained organizational sources, procedures, skills and decisions connected to the agents | Show its construction and connection before the tower's floors light; permissions and tests establish the task's real operating boundary |

A story uses one of these words only after showing what it means.
