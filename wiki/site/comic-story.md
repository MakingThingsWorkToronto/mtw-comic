# Comic Story (Comic B)

## Branded borders and margins

All comics use brand teal `#1a9ba1` for panel frames, gutters, inset separator bands and page margins, including loaders, desktop, phone and fallback views. White, off-white and sampled paper are never used for these surfaces. Approved speech-balloon fills and lettering colours stay separate. Reference geometry and texture remain; paper sampling cannot change the branded separator. This supersedes the earlier white/printed-paper separator treatment (Matt, 2026-10-09).

## Layered comic reader

The reader supports an ordered full-page raster stack. `scene.layers` declares `{id, src, role, opacity?}` with roles `background`, `humans`, `agents`, `props` or `light`. Complete PNG/WebP planes share the same aspect and coordinates; foregrounds retain genuine alpha. The loader decodes all planes before installation and falls back to retained `scene.art` when a plane is missing or mismatched. Fallback recovery does not establish layered acceptance.

Per-box `layerMotion` addresses plane IDs. `still`, `push`, `drift-left`, `drift-right`, `light`, `shadow` and digital-agent `projection` use the story clock, bounded to 2–8 beats and strength 0–1. Humans, evidence, lettering, paper, frames and gutters stay separate. The isolated archive `light` plane also supports `sprout`, revealing bottom-to-top in 2–16 authored steps. A dim archive below a revealing bright copy shows retained learning. Reduced motion shows the complete static archive and recognizable digital treatment. The step count does not prove independently drawn floor geometry or match a dedicated reference sheet automatically.

`panel.pageArt` contains the complete source crop in a desktop lettering reserve; `phoneArt` reserves a separately authored phone area. Every plane and the flattened fallback use the same crop placement. Dense process explanations are split into larger causal pages before type is reduced. Inspect actual completed balloons, faces, hands and operator-facing evidence on each desktop page and every phone sheet. Fine screen copy can remain too small even when the full image fits; this needs its own evidence view or responsive layout correction.

These capabilities are separate from ordered images within a box, which still require a declarative player path before use. The [paired layer craft](../../.codex/skills/mtw-comic-story/references/scene-layers.md), [exact visual gate](../../.codex/skills/mtw-comic-story/references/visual-review.md) and [capture/playback checks](../../.codex/skills/mtw-comic-story/references/checks.md) own production. Canonical records retain raw failed art, corrected planes, source-last import receipts and actual native observations; imported artwork, reader integration, hearing, publication and Matt’s approval remain distinct.

All four illustrated issues follow cast → frozen story/scene plan → inspected planes → motion/visual-grounded score → reviewed exact speech. The [delivery gap inventory](comic-delivery-gaps.md) tracks unfinished requirements; the [next-three task](../comics-library/records/next-three-layered-comic-task-20261010.md) retains the conversion handoff. Cached exact matching speech is reusable; the rejected narrator remains retained outside playback. New paid speech stays behind the word, casting and usage gates.

## The premise

Everyone has chat now, and it's making a mess. The damage comes from many people producing random, non-standard output, with nothing validating it and too much trust in AI. **Claude and Codex** are the heroes, but only when they borrow **human judgement**. Each decision, standard, correction or "no" from a person goes into their **second brain**, and the more judgement it holds, the stronger they get: **the second brain is their superpower.** The **operators** (the people who own the work) and the **users** are heroes too, because they hold the gates and validate every step. **Matt is the mentor**, and he steers.

The story is serious, adult and dark in both look and story. The feel is *Zack Snyder's Justice League*: an ensemble assembling against a threat, with slow, heavy hero beats. Sarcasm is a character's dry edge, never a gag. Exaggeration is allowed when it serves the story. Every company in the comic has an invented name. Stories may draw on Matt's resume, but no real client, employer or firm can be identified.

### Art

- **High-resolution image art (Matt, 2026-09-29):** the page art is Codex's drawing kept as a sharp raster at least 2x the screen's pixel density, keeping every hatch and dot. Balloons, borders, gutters and lettering stay crisp vector. The SVG trace pipeline remains in the skill as an option.
- **Style:** 1980s hand-inked line with feathered hatching, spotted blacks, dot screens and flat print colour. A modern digital-illustration look fails, and so does anything resembling 1990s computer graphics.
- **Darkness:** black and teal, matching the site, with 55–65% black ink and colour only where light falls. The cinematography leans dark: low-key light and silhouettes.
- **Whole pages:** Codex draws each page as one image from a layout template taken from a reference page (see `wiki/image-library/assets/scenes/comic-b/calibration/`). The engine reveals regions of that art. The art owns the box outlines.
- **Characters** hold their likeness and consistency through fixed model sheets and inputs. Inspect each appearance against its sheet (and, for Matt, his photo), and against the exact story action. Do not substitute a numerical style score for recognisable identity.

### The page

- **Box fill and quality (Matt, 2026-10-11):** Artwork fills each box in desktop, phone and reduced-motion presentation, without black sidebars or stretched proportions. Choose a responsive composition when a fill crop would remove required subjects/evidence. Check true retained source dimensions against its displayed crop and device pixel ratio; supply a higher-resolution source when scaling would soften the result. - **Reference page rhythm:** 6–8 boxes as in the original references, with no even proportional grids except one rapid run per issue. - **Box borders** are crisp, clean ruled lines, never rough.
- **Paper (Matt, 2026-09-29, the standard):** the page has an outer paper margin as well as the gutters between boxes.
